VERSION 0.5.123
Base: 0.5.122
Changes: Correct polar surface thermodynamics exposed by the Thermal Imager by separating radiating surface-skin temperature from ocean SST and restoring Antarctica-like continental cooling.
  - add a distinct surfaceSkinTemp field: open ocean still reports SST, while sea-ice-covered water exposes the temperature of the top ice surface rather than the near-freezing mixed layer below it
  - sea-ice skin temperature is thermally coupled to basal SST through ice thickness, follows local air temperature and receives an additional bounded radiative drop in polar darkness; thick winter ice can therefore be tens of degrees colder than the water beneath it
  - the visible/radiating skin temperature is fed back into local outgoing-longwave calculations on the next fixed Weather Core tick instead of treating basal SST as the radiating surface beneath sea ice
  - Thermal Imager GPU publication now packs surfaceSkinTemp when available, so the IR view no longer paints frozen polar oceans at about -1.8 C merely because the underlying seawater is pinned near its freezing point
  - user-facing broad surface-temperature diagnostics now read the visible/radiating skin field; ocean physics keeps seaSurfaceTemp as the authoritative mixed-layer reservoir
  - add absolute terrain lapse-rate cooling at 6.0 K/km up to 5 km, outside the zero-mean geographic heat-redistribution term, so broad high plateaus no longer lose their elevation cooling when a latitude-band mean is removed
  - add a bounded stable polar boundary-layer/inversion correction over continental high latitudes, strengthened by snow/land ice and polar darkness and disabled on genuinely hot worlds
  - the new polar land term is bootstrapped on world creation and maintained as a slow physical surface-budget forcing rather than a shader-only colour correction
  - an Earth-like high polar plateau around 3.5 km receives roughly 30+ K of additional surface cooling beyond the old zonal baseline, allowing Antarctica/Vostok-like -45..-55 C states and colder winter excursions while low polar oceans remain moderated by sea ice and ocean heat capacity
Regression: polar-surface-thermodynamics.test.js validates plateau lapse cooling, stable polar inversion, thermally decoupled sea-ice skin temperature, skin-driven OLR and GPU thermography. Both builders guard module order around atmospheric transport and fog GPU publication.
Snapshot: 0.5.122 remains the rollback point before explicit polar surface-skin thermodynamics.
