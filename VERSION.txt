VERSION 0.5.79
Base: 0.5.78
Changes: Close-orbit equilibrium physics, cumulative volatile-retention proxy, immediate Weather Core rebase after extreme BASE-forcing changes, and sharper irregular continental ice margins.
Root cause: the 627 °C ceiling was not physical. climate-regimes.js deliberately clamped its ordinary-climate solver to 900 K, and 900 K is exactly 626.85 °C. In addition, persistent Weather Core kept the previous habitable-zone thermal reservoirs and relaxed surface temperature on a multi-hour simulated timescale after a radical orbit change. The 0.5.78 hot-water/biology shader gates were therefore correct but often received stale temperate surface state.
Climate: the final close-orbit equilibrium can now exceed the retired 900 K ceiling up to a 2200 K safety range. Once an old close-in rocky world has lost most volatiles, absorbed stellar power is recomputed with a dry rocky Bond-albedo scaffold rather than stale ocean/cloud/ice albedo. For a G star at 1 L☉ and 0.065 AU (~234 S⊕), an Earth-gravity volatile-poor world settles around ~1000–1100 K depending albedo/greenhouse instead of sticking at exactly 900 K.
Atmospheric evolution: a compact cumulative retention proxy now depends on incident stellar flux, planet age and escape velocity. It is explicitly an equilibrium/evolution scaffold, NOT an instantaneous hydrodynamic escape solver. Earth/HZ and Venus-like bolometric fluxes are left essentially unchanged; very old, very close rocky planets progressively lose H/He first, then water, then ordinary heavy atmospheric columns unless high escape velocity protects them. Renderer atmosphere density follows retained pressure, so a stripped world no longer keeps an Earth-blue halo merely because its original gas sliders were Earth-like.
Water: the conserved H₂O slider remains a BASE cause, but downstream equilibrium reservoirs see the long-term retained fraction. Extreme old close-orbit worlds therefore cannot keep an Earth ocean after the orbit has made long-term water retention negligible. Moving the BASE controls back outward restores the available reservoir because the UI describes alternative equilibrium worlds rather than simulating one irreversible orbital migration history.
Weather persistence: normal parameter tuning preserves live weather. Only a major equilibrium jump (>70 K, >2.5× stellar flux or >4× pressure) rebuilds Weather Core from the new BASE state. Moving a temperate world directly to ~234 S⊕ therefore stops showing stale green valleys and blue water for tens of real seconds.
Surface: 0.5.78's existing two-sided biological gate, critical-water shutdown and dry mineral ocean-bed rendering are retained. They now receive the correct hot state immediately. At ~1000 K the world is baked/dehydrated and sterile, but not necessarily a global magma ocean: basaltic/silicate whole-surface melting generally requires still higher temperatures. True vitrified/magma material should be reserved for hotter regimes rather than painting a ~800 °C average surface as liquid glass.
Cryosphere display: land snow/ice still comes only from physical Weather Core coverage, but its visual transfer is now nearly binary across a narrow seed-stable sub-cell threshold. Dense continental ice stays opaque; the boundary moves with multi-scale seamless edge noise, forming bays/tongues instead of a broad translucent fuzzy fringe. Sea-pack concentration keeps its gradual physical display.
Regression: extreme-orbit-regressions.test.js guards Earth-flux retention, severe volatile depletion at ~234 S⊕ for an old Earth-gravity planet, temperatures above 900 K, immediate stale-weather rebase, build ordering, and crisp continental-ice display while leaving sea pack ice gradual.
Validation: confirm visible stamp v0.5.79. Recheck seed 40491874 at G / 1.00 L☉ / ~0.065 AU: the mean climate must exceed 627 °C, retained ordinary atmosphere/water must collapse strongly for an old Earth-like-gravity world, and green/blue surface biology/liquid water must disappear without waiting for the old Weather Core. On a cold Earth-like world, the Antarctic/continental cap should have a sharper, more irregular opaque boundary rather than a milky translucent edge.
Retained: 0.5.78 extreme phase renderer, tectonic/volcanic stripe repair and Android viewport locking; 0.5.77 deep-freeze water closure; 0.5.75 dense ice display; 0.5.74 seam-aware cubemap reconstruction.
