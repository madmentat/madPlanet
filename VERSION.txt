VERSION 0.5.129
Base: 0.5.128
Changes: Make visible polar ice thermodynamically consistent with the final rendered surface temperature and reduce over-solid thin sea-ice caps.
  - close the snow/land-ice/sea-ice phase balance once more after late atmospheric/ocean heat transport and polar-continent modules, so heat introduced after the original cryosphere step is spent on latent melting before the final surface is published
  - while snow or glacier ice remains, late positive sensible heat clamps landSurfaceTemp to the 0 C melting boundary until the available ice mass is actually consumed
  - while bulk sea ice remains, late positive sensible heat melts thickness first and keeps SST at the seawater freezing boundary (~-1.8 C); only after all ice has melted may leftover heat warm open water above that point
  - republish cryosphere coverage and polar surfaceSkinTemp after the late phase closure, so fog GPU, thermal imagery and the physical probe all receive the same final thermodynamic state
  - widen thickness-to-sea-ice-area e-folding from the old display shortcut to 0.35 m: ~10 cm new ice now leaves broad leads, while ~1 m still gives a compact pack; deep supercooling can still close the surface through the existing thermodynamic skin floor
  - Thermal mode samples the exact dense binary cryosphere display mask and clamps visible snow/ice pixels to <=0 C before mountain/lava refinements, preventing linear temperature filtering from painting a white ice pixel at +2..+4 C near a coarse Weather Core boundary
  - retain energy conservation: the new late closure consumes only sensible heat already present in the final land/ocean reservoirs and converts the corresponding snow/ice mass to liquid; it does not invent cooling or delete H2O
Regression: cryosphere-phase-consistency.test.js checks late land/sea latent-heat closure, thin-vs-compact sea-ice area response, removal of fully melted thin ice and exact Thermal/cryosphere phase consistency.
Snapshot: 0.5.128 remains the rollback point before the final-tick cryosphere phase closure and less aggressive thin-ice coverage.
